Governing essay

Finality Before Inquiry

The case as an ordering violation, not a hindsight complaint.

Finality must follow inquiry. It must not precede it.

The cleanest way to understand this case is not as a dispute about diagnosis. It is a dispute about order. A medical system may be uncertain. It may act provisionally. It may make a working diagnosis, choose caution, defer treatment, order further tests, and acknowledge that time may clarify what is not yet clear. All of that is legitimate.

What it cannot safely do is behave as though the question is settled while the process required to settle it remains incomplete.

The patient did not enter hospital as a clean Parkinson’s case. He entered through a foot-centred gait failure with neurological signs. The referral preserved uncertainty. It identified abnormal gait, stiffness, increased tone, brisk reflexes, severe foot pathology, deteriorating function, and the possibility of Parkinsonism or another neurological disorder. The hospital accepted that problem. It accepted a duty to explain the relationship between the gait, the feet, and the neurological signs.

That inquiry had to come first. Finality had to wait.

The discharge summary shows that finality did not wait. It declared Parkinson’s Disease while the record still contained features that should have kept the case open: youth, atypicality, limited tremor, dystonic-looking gait, unresolved feet, brisk reflexes, genetic possibility, deliberate deferral of levodopa, possible future regret, and a statement that the clinician was not a movement-disorders neurologist.

Those facts do not prove that the final diagnosis was impossible. They prove that the diagnostic sequence was incomplete.

A working impression can coexist with incompleteness. A final diagnosis cannot, at least not without explicit qualification. If the record says suspected young-onset Parkinsonism, atypical, pending further discrimination, uncertainty is preserved. If the record says Primary Diagnosis: Parkinson’s Disease, certifies it, and moves the patient into administrative reliance, uncertainty is no longer governing. It becomes background.

The institution converted uncertainty into background and certainty into structure.

This is why the case should not be argued as hindsight. Later genetic confirmation may illuminate the record, but it does not create the original defect. The original defect is visible in 2017. The record itself shows that the case was not fully discriminated. The foundation is the order of acts: entry through a foot-centred neurological problem; Parkinsonism recognised; atypicality recorded; levodopa withheld; possible regret recorded; non-specialist status recorded; feet unresolved; Parkinson’s Disease finalised and certified.

The most important question is not whether Parkinsonism could have been suspected. It could. The question is what the institution was entitled to do with that suspicion. Suspicion is not finality. Suspicion permits inquiry. Finality closes it.

The same structure appears in every hinge. With levodopa: if the discriminator was withheld, uncertainty remained. With expertise: if the clinician was not a movement-disorders neurologist in a young, atypical, foot-centred, levodopa-undiscriminated case, that limitation should have altered the pathway. With the feet: if the initiating problem was not integrated, falsified, or superseded, it remained governing. With certification: if a statutory diagnosis was created before inquiry caught up, suspicion became reliance without entitlement. With the DAT scan: if the scan arrived after discharge and certification, it was confirmation after closure, not inquiry before finality.

The omitted protest belongs in the same structure. The patient objected, in substance, that the feet had not been addressed. That was a direct challenge to the completeness of the admission. If that protest was not recorded, the discharge summary lost the one real-time patient objection that exposed the ordering failure.

This case is therefore not about demanding perfection from medicine. It is about demanding correct ordering from institutions. Doctors do not have to know everything at once. They do have to preserve uncertainty when uncertainty remains material. They do not have to treat immediately with every possible medication. They do have to recognise when withholding a treatment also withholds diagnostic knowledge.

Do not close what you have not completed. Do not certify what you have not sufficiently discriminated. Do not turn a working hypothesis into durable administrative fact while the initiating problem remains unresolved.

Misdiagnosis is too small a word. The issue is not merely that a label may have been wrong or incomplete. The issue is that the system lost the right to treat the label as final when it did. The harm began not with a single erroneous word, but with a sequence inversion.

Inquiry was still open.

Finality arrived anyway.

That is the case.
