Entry artefact

GP Referral — 29 June 2017

The duty-defining artefact: a foot-centred gait failure accepted for neurological assessment.

The case did not enter as an abstraction. It entered through gait, function, and the feet.

The GP referral is the entry document. Its importance is that it fixes the problem the hospital accepted: abnormal gait, difficulty initiating movement, increased tone and stiffness, brisk reflexes, work-threatening deterioration, and bilateral foot pathology described as feet “not in a good state.”

The referral did not assert a settled diagnosis. It queried Parkinsonian or other neurological disease and preserved diagnostic openness. That makes the document structurally powerful. It defines the scope of inquiry before the hospital record begins to reorganise the case.

Once accepted, the receiving service had to explain, falsify, or supersede the referral-defined problem. The feet could not simply become background. They were the entry signal.

Where, in the subsequent record, was the foot-centred problem resolved, superseded, or falsified?

This artefact should be read alongside The Feet Were Not Incidental and Finality Before Inquiry.
